# VST — Investment Thesis

## Style
Thematic Specialist / AI Power Demand / Utility Rotation

## Core Thesis
Buying a merchant-and-retail power operator with direct exposure to the most important second-order effect of the AI boom: a sharp rise in electricity demand from data centers. Vistra offers a different utility profile than regulated compounders like NextEra because it has more direct earnings torque to tightening power markets, higher load growth, and improving capacity economics. The thesis is that AI-linked demand growth, particularly in power-hungry regions, can support stronger cash generation and better equity upside than a more fully valued defensive utility. The key question is whether the market has already priced in too much of that AI power narrative and whether power-market volatility will swamp the secular demand tailwind. Judgment: worth a seed position, not a full commitment.

## Entry Context
Initial purchase at $159.00 for a 0.64% seed position. The entry is being made after a technical reset rather than at momentum highs, which fits a rotation framework better than chasing an extended winner. This is not a classic defensive utility purchase; it is a thematic power-demand bet funded from within the utility sleeve. The role of the position is to gain measured exposure to AI-driven electricity demand without treating the thesis as fully proven on day one.

## Bull Case
- **AI Load Growth:** Large-scale AI training and inference clusters require enormous and persistent electricity demand, and Vistra is positioned to benefit from tighter power markets as hyperscale and data-center demand expands.
- **Higher Earnings Torque:** Compared with lower-volatility regulated utilities, Vistra has more operating leverage to improving wholesale power pricing, reserve margins, and capacity-market strength.
- **Rotation Upside:** This trade rotates capital from a name already in a documented trim zone into a name with more cyclical upside if the AI-power theme broadens beyond obvious winners.
- **Capital Allocation Flexibility:** If management continues to balance generation economics, retail exposure, and shareholder returns well, the stock can participate in both earnings growth and multiple support.
- **Earnings torque is confirmed, and it sits where this thesis said it would (Q2 2026).** Generation adjusted EBITDA $994M vs $593M a year earlier (+68%); Retail $773M vs $756M (+2%). Total Q2 adjusted EBITDA $1.767B, +30% YoY. The operating leverage is real and it is concentrated in generation, not retail — which is the specific claim under "Higher Earnings Torque" above.
- **Capital returns are running ahead of the stated plan.** ~171M shares retired since Nov 2021 at an average of ~$38; >$6.5B returned against a $6B-through-2026 target, hit early; $1.2B authorization remaining, expected exhausted no later than end-2027. Investment grade at two of three agencies, targeting mid-IG at all three.

## Key Risks
- **Theme Crowding:** The AI power-demand story is now well known. If expectations outrun actual load conversion timelines, the stock may de-rate before fundamentals catch up.
  - **This risk is live as of Q2 2026 — the file called it correctly.** ERCOT forwards are meaningfully below the 2025-10-31 basis used for the 2027 midpoint range; management maintained $7.4–7.8B but stated they are "trending towards the lower end." Burke placed load hookup in 2027–2028 and said "2026 being soft is not that big of a surprise to us." Management's own read on the demand side is that the ERCOT interconnect queue, at times expressed as >400 GW, is realistically **12–15 GW by 2030** — "overstated by more than 20x." The timing gap between narrative and load conversion is the thing to watch, and it is now management-confirmed rather than inferred.
- **Power Market Volatility:** Weather, commodity inputs, hedging outcomes, and regional pricing can create uneven earnings, making this a rougher hold than a regulated utility compounder.
  - **Named mechanism (added 2026-08-07): battery storage is compressing scarcity pricing in the bridging hours.** Post-ECRS battery buildout now covers the solar-to-wind gap in ERCOT. Per management, 2026-07-22 needed ~25 GWh of battery discharge against ~31 GWh available; the day cleared **$57/MWh** when a couple thousand MW of difference in thermal, load or wind "could have cleared closer to $400 or $500." Battery returns are running ~1/5 of investor expectations and battery queues are slowing, which should ease this over time — but it is the specific reason ERCOT scarcity pricing is soft, and it is a supply-side dynamic this file previously covered only as generic "volatility." ERCOT YTD wholesale is ~$30/MWh, flat year over year.
- **Helix / non-power capital at risk (new exposure, 2026-08-07).** Vistra is a founding investor in **Helix Digital Infrastructure** alongside KKR, NVIDIA and the Kuwait Investment Authority, committing **up to $1B over time, with anything above $500M gated on milestones**, plus preferred-power-partner status. This is a different risk than the thesis was underwritten on: Vistra taking **equity risk in digital infrastructure**, not selling power into it. It is optional deal-by-deal and management frames it as an additional channel rather than a requirement, and Vistra states it will only participate at its standard mid-teens levered return threshold. But it competes for the same capital as the buyback credited under Bull Case, and capital-call timing is explicitly unpredictable ("we cannot predict the amount or timing of any potential capital calls"). **[BILL] — decide whether this belongs in the thesis as an upside channel, a risk, or both. It is currently recorded only as a risk because it is new and unproven.**
- **Long-End Rate Sensitivity:** Vistra is a long-duration cash-flow asset. Live pressure often shows up through the long end of the curve (discount-rate / multiple compression) even when near-term power fundamentals are intact — a risk this file previously omitted.
- **Policy and Regulatory Risk:** Merchant generators can benefit from scarcity, but they also sit in politically sensitive markets where rule changes, intervention, or shifting capacity frameworks can alter returns.
- **Opportunity Cost:** Selling a stronger technical name to buy a weaker one can backfire if the downtrend persists longer than expected.
