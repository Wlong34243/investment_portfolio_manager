# APA — Investment Thesis

## Core Thesis (Discounted E&P with Suriname Optionality)

I am buying a deeply discounted exploration and production company that the market is pricing as if its two most interesting assets — the Suriname offshore discovery and the Permian basin position — are worth zero. APA Corp (formerly Apache) is a mid-cap E&P with a geographically diversified asset base (Permian, North Sea, Egypt), a track record of capital discipline, and a potentially company-making offshore oil discovery in Suriname Block 58 that the market has largely abandoned given the long lead time and capital intensity.

This is a thematic position: I am buying oil-price torque and optionality, not a compounder. The thesis is that APA is deeply cheap versus proved reserves and production cash flows at mid-cycle oil prices, with a material probability-weighted call option on Suriname development that the stock price essentially ignores.

## Entry Context

Cost basis: ~$3,189 total. APA has been among the worst-performing E&P stocks over the past two years — de-rated on Suriname development uncertainty, Egypt political risk, North Sea depletion concerns, and the sector-wide valuation compression as oil prices retreated from 2022 highs. The bear case is largely priced in.

- Thematic frame: this is not a high-conviction quality business like XOM. It is a deeply discounted asset play where the stock price implies a pessimistic scenario across all three producing basins and zero for Suriname. I am betting that at least one of those assumptions is wrong.
- Size discipline: intentionally capped at 3% ceiling; this is a lottery-ticket-plus-yield position, not a core holding.

## Bull Case

- **Suriname Block 58:** TotalEnergies is the operator and has confirmed multi-billion barrel resource estimates. A final investment decision (FID) and development approval would be a significant rerating catalyst, adding proved reserves and long-duration production that current APA equity prices do not reflect.
- **Permian free cash flow:** Even at $65–70/bbl WTI, Permian assets generate meaningful FCF. APA has been using that cash to reduce debt and buy back stock at historically cheap multiples.
- **Oil price recovery:** APA has high operational leverage to oil prices — a move from $65 to $80/bbl WTI has an outsized impact on earnings and FCF versus more hedged peers.
- **Capital discipline:** Management has reduced the dividend to a sustainable level and shifted capital toward buybacks and debt reduction rather than growth capex at cycle highs.
- **Deep value vs. NAV:** At current prices, APA trades at a material discount to proved-reserve NAV using conservative oil price assumptions.

## Key Risks

- **Suriname delay or abandonment:** If TotalEnergies decides against an FID or Suriname blocks encounter technical challenges, the most important catalyst evaporates.
- **Egypt political and operational risk:** Egypt assets generate meaningful production but are subject to political risk and PSA contract terms that can compress economics.
- **Oil price decline:** APA's balance sheet and free cash flow are materially sensitive to oil prices; a sustained move to $55/bbl or below pressures all three producing regions simultaneously.
- **North Sea depletion:** North Sea assets are declining naturally; without reinvestment, this base will erode.

## Scaling State & Priority

- Next step: hold starter position; add below $18 on oil-cycle fear or Suriname headline noise if reserves thesis remains intact.
- Rotation priority: high; this is a speculative thematic with a clear exit trigger (Suriname FID announced or definitively abandoned).
