# JEPI — Investment Thesis

## Style
Low-Beta Income / Equity Ballast

## Core Thesis
Low-beta equity exposure. JEPI holds a defensive large-cap equity book and sells covered calls against it, converting upside volatility into monthly income. It is the equity sleeve that lets the portfolio stay invested through choppy markets without the full drawdown profile of the index, and its income stream is part of the strategic-cash / dry-powder posture rather than a growth bet.

## Entry Context
Held as the largest single position (~7%) precisely because it is not a directional bet. It is ballast: the covered-call structure caps upside in strong rallies, which is an accepted cost for dampened volatility and monthly cash flow.

## Bull Case
- Dampens portfolio beta while keeping equity market participation.
- Monthly income funds small-step rotations without forcing sales elsewhere.
- ELN/covered-call structure benefits from elevated volatility regimes when premiums are rich.

## Key Risks
- Capped upside: systematically lags in sustained bull runs; a long momentum market makes this the portfolio's biggest drag.
- Income is taxed largely as ordinary income in a taxable account — tax-inefficient relative to qualified dividends.
- Not a bond substitute: still falls in equity drawdowns, just less.

## Scaling State
next_step: hold

## Rotation Priority
priority: low

## Exit Conditions
- The role changes: if the portfolio needs more growth and less ballast, trim toward JPIE/bonds or broad equity rather than holding both.
- Sustained distribution decline alongside index-lagging performance (structure not delivering its side of the trade).
