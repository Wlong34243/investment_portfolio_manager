# GILD — Investment Thesis

## Core Thesis (HIV Durable Cash Engine + Oncology Option)

I am buying a deeply profitable pharmaceutical franchise that the market persistently undervalues because its core HIV business is "mature." Gilead generates $25B+ in annual revenue and over $7B in free cash flow, predominantly from a durable HIV treatment and prevention portfolio (Biktarvy, Descovy, Sunlenca) that has near-monopoly pricing power in a chronically treated patient population. The thesis is simple: the HIV franchise generates a multi-decade annuity at a below-market multiple, and the market is giving me the oncology pipeline (Trodelvy, Livdelzi, lenacapavir for HIV prevention) for close to free.

This is a classic BORING play — a fear-discounted, cash-rich franchise where I am paid to wait. The fear is pipeline durability and competition from long-acting injectables; the reality is that Gilead's own long-acting product (lenacapavir for PrEP) is among the most significant HIV prevention advances in a decade, with regulatory momentum and a massive underserved TAM.

## Entry Context & Valuation

Cost basis: ~$4,573 total. GILD entered during a period of uncertainty around the lenacapavir PrEP regulatory path and residual skepticism about Trodelvy's label expansion. At these levels the stock trades at ~12x forward earnings — a deep discount to the S&P 500 and to specialty pharma peers with comparable FCF profiles.

- BORING frame: low P/E, high FCF yield, long profitability history, and a fear narrative (pipeline risk, HIV market share) that creates a durable discount. I am collecting a ~3% dividend yield while I wait.
- The dividend is well-covered by FCF; GILD has raised it for several consecutive years and does meaningful buybacks on top.

## Bull Case

- **Lenacapavir for PrEP:** If approved for HIV prevention (biannual injection vs. daily pill), lenacapavir addresses a massive underserved market globally. FDA decision timing is a near-term catalyst.
- **HIV durability:** The switch franchise (Biktarvy + Sunlenca) continues to generate patient migration from older regimens. Generic threats to core HIV are years away given patent coverage.
- **Trodelvy in breast cancer:** Label expansions into HR+/HER2- breast cancer and lung cancer are in trials; a positive readout significantly expands the addressable market beyond current TNBC approval.
- **Livdelzi (liver disease):** Gilead's primary biliary cholangitis drug (Livdelzi) is growing rapidly in a space with limited competition.
- **Capital return:** $7B+ in FCF supports a combination of buybacks and dividend growth; the balance sheet is investment grade and leverage is moderate.

## Behavioral Guardrails & What I Watch

- Drawdown tolerance: 20–25% from cost is within thesis; HIV franchise cash flows are visible multi-year.
- Watch: Biktarvy market share (monthly script data), lenacapavir PrEP FDA timeline, Trodelvy enrollment and interim readouts, and quarterly FCF per share.
- I will not overreact to single trial misses in secondary indications — the HIV franchise is the investment; oncology is the option.

## Hard Exit Conditions

1. HIV franchise revenue declines accelerate beyond low single digits for more than two consecutive years, suggesting structural share loss or generic entry ahead of schedule.
2. The dividend is cut without a clear capital allocation rationale (would signal FCF deterioration more severe than thesis).
3. A cheaper, superior oral HIV regimen from a competitor achieves meaningful formulary access at the expense of Biktarvy or Sunlenca.
4. Lenacapavir for PrEP fails FDA approval and Trodelvy shows no label expansion progress — removing both near-term catalysts simultaneously.

## Scaling State & Priority

- Next step: hold at current 0.67%; add on weakness below $90 if HIV franchise metrics are intact.
- Rotation priority: low; this is a slow-moving income-plus-optionality position.
